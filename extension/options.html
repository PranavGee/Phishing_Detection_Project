    How it works

    A gradient-boosting model, trained on 11,430 labelled URLs, reads nine structural properties of an address — its length, the length of the hostname, whether it hides a raw IP or a long hex token, and the number of . - @ ? & | characters. It never loads the page, reads its content, or sends anything over the network.

    That also means it can be wrong in both directions: a plain-looking phishing URL can slip through, and an unusually long legitimate URL can be flagged. Treat a warning as a reason to look carefully at the address bar, not as proof.
